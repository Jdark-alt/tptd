# TPTD (Two Player Tower Defence): notes for Claude Code

TPTD, Two Player Tower Defence (called Siegeline until v13), is an asymmetric 1v1 tower defense played in the browser. One player defends the keep with walls and towers. The other attacks with an army they design and choreograph. The game started in a long claude.ai chat and moves here now. ## Working with the user

- The user designs the game and playtests it with friends, online and in pass-and-play. Their reports are the ground truth ("attackers never win without charges", "one maxed unit can't be stopped"). Take them seriously, then measure with the simulators before changing numbers.
- For balance, they like hearing your opinion with data behind it: what you ran, what it showed, and what you'd change. Then they decide. They're fine with action when they've asked for it.
- Explain in plain words: what the player sees and feels, not code internals.
- They deploy by pushing to this repo. Keep the game a single `index.html` that works opened as a file, on GitHub Pages and on Render.
- Phones matter. Every UI change must work in portrait (the board transposes) and at 390 px wide.

## Look and feel (v13)

The user asked for a minimal, designed look in the game's tech style, with no clutter and no unnecessary text. Keep new UI in this language:

- **Type:** Doto (dot-matrix) only for the wordmark and big numerals (`--f-display`); Geist Mono for labels, buttons, tabs and small numbers (`--f-ui`, usually uppercase, letter-spaced, 10–12 px); Geist for prose (`--f-body`). Loaded from Google Fonts; the fallbacks are system mono and sans.
- **Colour:** near-black field (`--ink`, `--slate`), hairline borders (`--line`, `--line-2`), cyan for the defender (`--cool`), orange for the attacker (`--warm`), gold only for the keep (`--keep`), violet for tower charges (`--chain`). `--side` follows whose turn it is.
- **Buttons on the Wave step (v22.1, at the user's request: "visually separate the text from the buttons"):** what you can press is a filled box (`#17202a`, a `#2f3945` border) in Geist, sentence case, 12 px (`.wvb`, `.wv-tools .wvs button`); what only names things is 9.5 px Geist Mono capitals in `--muted`. Keep that split when adding controls there.
- **Shape:** 4–6 px radii, 1 px lines, flat fills, no glows or gradients on controls (two exceptions the user asked for: a slider at the top of its range glows and shimmers, v21.1, and the wave plan's play button pulses until first pressed, v20.2). Accent lines (a 2 px top or left edge, the active tab's underline) carry the side colour.
- **Board:** a flat field with a dot at each cell corner, a hairline frame with corner ticks (dashed in the attacker's last-seen view). Walls are flat slate blocks joined by narrow links (new ones brighter with a cyan edge); towers are a hairline square with a turret; bridges an orange-railed deck; the keep a gold square with a diamond; terrain stippled or hatched. Hot tiles (the hint) are a soft lime square with a slow pulse, a colour nothing else on the board uses. Canvas text is Geist Mono via `pill()`.
- **Copy:** one short line where a paragraph used to be. Helper notes on phones hide unless they carry `.show`.
- **The start screen (v25, the user: "make it absolutely stunning … add some beautiful custom graphics … have the game stand out visually"):** its drawings are inline SVG in the board's own language (the dot grid, slate walls joined by links, hairline towers with a turret and a dashed range ring, units in the army colours, the gold keep), animated with SMIL (`animateMotion` along the route, turrets turning, tracers) and paused while the screen is hidden or the player asks for reduced motion. The cards carry the brand's split cyan/orange line along their top (the `.mark`'s two colours), lift 2 px on hover, and their Play buttons are the light `.ready.neutral` with an arrow. The settings' pressed segments are light, as before. The hero's fade and the card hover's shadow are the only gradients and shadows, both decorative.
- **Light and pixels (v30, the user: "incorporate lighting … use the pixelated futuristic theme to just create a blocker … don't try to make them real"):** the board is lit by its own light map (see "Art"), dark where nothing shines; terrain is drawn as designed tiles and dots in the dot-matrix spirit of the wordmark, never as natural rock or earth: a blocker's square tiles, a halftone for rough ground, perforated floor plates. Units were vehicles with a face in v30; since v31 they are abstract shapes (see below), never vehicles or creatures. Keep new board art to that: light from the top left, a silhouette first, pixels and tiles rather than imitation textures.
- **The board's art (v29, the user: "custom assets … being able to tell towers and enemies apart … go all in"):** the board isn't a control, so it may glow: projectiles, muzzle flashes, explosions and lights are added with `'lighter'` (`glow`), everything else stays flat and dark. Light comes from the top left and every baked highlight is symmetric about the diagonal (top and left edges lit, bottom and right dark, diagonal gradients), so the transposed portrait board lights the same way. Cyan stays the defender's (tower rims, plinths), orange and the warm army colours the attacker's, gold the keep's, violet sabotage's; Frost is `--frost`, Venom `--poison`, Tesla `--chain`. A new kind of thing on the board gets a silhouette of its own before a colour of its own. Damage numbers are Geist Mono over a dark stroke; words over the board (BRIDGE, SAPPER DOWN) are small dark pills with a coloured edge. Controls keep the rules above (flat, hairlines); the only new control is the speaker, styled like `?`.
- **Towers (v31, the user's spec: "everything on a tower visible from its appearance, consistently"):** a tower's look is fixed layers, never "the last one added": the silhouette is the attack type, each setting has one slot drawn the same way on every silhouette, and an element adds its colour to its own slot only. A new tower setting gets a slot of its own on all three silhouettes.
- **Units (v31, the user's spec: "shape = speed only … abstract, minimal, futuristic … no vehicles", then "be bold"):** a unit's shape is its speed step and nothing else (eight shapes, slow ones heavy and inert, fast ones unmistakably fast, every one with a clear front); size is health, colour the army; trails, hover height and pulse grow with speed; armour, regeneration and resists are markers drawn the same way on every shape. A new unit setting gets a marker, never a new shape.
- **Surfaces and towers (v32, the user's spec: "modern, animated … soft gradients, subtle depth, backgrounds that blend into the backdrop"; "more serious, more threatening towers"):** pop-ups and the side panels' cards are surfaces (`.surf` and the rules in the "v32: surfaces" block): a gradient over translucent slate with a backdrop blur, so a blurred slice of the board shows through, grain, a light along the top edge, a deep soft shadow and a faint glow in the side's colour; they rise in (sections a beat apart) and fade out (`exitGhost`), never with reduced motion. Controls keep the flat v13 look above. Towers are gunmetal with the element colours as muted accents (lights, rims, the venom canister), Frost a cold steel blue; heavy barrels with real muzzles, chamfered housings, armour seams; no tower is the brightest thing on the board until it fires.
- **The cards' scenes (v31):** built from the board's own sprites and the same light, seen through a camera; things stand (extruded, lit tops, sides shaded by the lamps), the camera keeps low and to the side and moves slowly. It's a menu, not a trailer.
- **Sound (v29; v32 weightier):** since v32 a shot is a crack over a low thump with a short tail (no glassy pings), the mortar's shell lands as an explosion, Frost and Venom are quiet layers under the shot, and the mix runs through a gentle low-pass and a compressor. Short and quiet for frequent things (a blaster's shot, a gatling's rattle, a click on every button), weightier for rare ones (a railgun's crack, a mortar's boom, a unit's pop, the keep's gong, a bridge's crash, a knock-out's zap, the wave's horn, the summary's chime, a fanfare or a fall at the end). Synthesised, never loud, capped in voices and per sound.
- **Name:** the player sees TPTD everywhere. Internal identifiers keep the old name on purpose (`window.siegeline`, `SIEGE_RELAY`, `SIEGE_PEER_OPTS`, `siege-p…` rooms, `siegeline-` peer ids), since tests, the relay and the artifact copy rely on them.

## Repo layout and deploy

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The entire game: HTML, CSS and JS in one file. No build step. |
| `server.js` | Relay server for online play. It also serves `index.html` at `/` (injecting `window.SIEGE_RELAY="same-origin"`), plus `/health`, WebSocket `/relay` and, since v30, `/music/<name>` (audio files from `music/` only). Keeps no game state. Since v26 a room takes 4 players (two seats and joiners waiting) and 8 watchers (`MAX_PLAYERS`, `MAX_WATCHERS`; a watcher says so when it joins, `w: 1`). |
| `package.json` | Root dependencies for Render: only `ws`. Don't add test tooling here (Render installs it). |
| `music/` | The menu music (v30): `menu.mp3` goes here (see its README). Pages serves it; `server.js` serves this folder's audio files (MP3, OGG, M4A, WAV, with ranges) and nothing else. Without the file the game plays a synthesised theme. The user's track is in; at their request it was trimmed to start 23 s in (2:22, 160 kbps, a 20 ms fade-in so the loop doesn't click). |
| `tools/` | Simulators and browser test suites (below), and since v25 `tools/art/start_art.py`, which draws the start screen's SVGs. Only used locally; Render and Pages ignore them. |
| `docs/specs/` | The user's specs for builds made from one (v19 on). |

- **Render** (free web service): Build `npm install`, Start `npm start`. Free instances sleep after 15 minutes without inbound traffic and take about a minute to wake. The page shows "Waking the game server" and retries for 90 s. Pages send a heartbeat every 15 s, so a match keeps the server awake.
- **GitHub Pages** serves the same `index.html`. Pages hosted elsewhere only use the relay if `const RELAY_HOST = ''` near the top of the script holds the Render URL. With it empty they fall back to PeerJS, which fails between different networks. The players currently use the Render link.
- **claude.ai artifact:** there is also a copy published as a Claude artifact, where online play uses the claude.ai room capability (`window.claude.use('room')`). That code path is inert elsewhere. Keep it working, but the repo is the source of truth now.
- **Branches:** `main` is what the friends play on Render. At the user's request `bridges` was merged into `main` with v13. `bridges` stays the branch GitHub Pages serves for testing; new work goes there first and reaches `main` only when the user says so. ### Determinism (do not break)

Online play sends only plans. Both devices then run the same wave and must get bit-identical results. Inside the wave simulation:

- Use `srand()`, never `Math.random`. It's seeded per match, epoch and round. `Math.random` is fine for visual effects only (which side a miss is drawn on, for one).
- Every shot fired rolls `srand()` exactly once (v19), hit or miss, even at 100% accuracy, so the stream depends only on the shots fired. Splash and chain hits don't roll. Before v19 only dodge rolled, so at 100% accuracy a wave without dodge plays exactly as in v18 (`test_local.py` checks it against the v18 build).
- Use `hyp()` (sqrt), never `Math.hypot`. No trig either; `atan2`/`cos`/`sin` are only for drawing angles.
- Step in fixed 1/60 s increments (`STEP`). The speed buttons run more steps per frame; they never change the step size.
- The map is part of the match: both devices derive its seed from the match code and epoch (`seedFor('map', code, ep)`), the defender's plan carries it as `ms`, and if they ever differ the defender's wins. Terrain is never randomised inside a wave, and `trial` reuses the match's map.
- Keep path costs integers. The route field is kept in 1/`FK` (64) units and each bridge cell costs one unit less: that breaks exact ties toward the bridge and never outweighs a real difference. With no bridges it plays exactly like v9.
- Rebuild state on both sides from the encoded plans (`beginOnlineWave` decodes both players' plans, its own included). Sabotage is priced and capped by the rules when a plan is decoded, each bridge gets its sapper's tile there (from `S.seenGrid` and `S.bridges`, which both devices share), and nothing in a decoded grid may stand on a bridge (`clearBridges`). A plan carries each sapper's launch time as an integer 0–1295.
- Sappers (v17) are units: they spawn from `buildSpawns` after the army (so a wave without sabotage draws the same random numbers as before), and everything they check (their tile on `routeAhead`, a tower's range, `inReach`) is integer route fields and plain arithmetic, no trig. A bridge laid mid-wave re-routes everyone through `recompute()`, as v10's start-of-wave bridges did. A knock-out doesn't (v19, from `main`): `dangerField` counts knocked-out towers too, so `sapRange` only silences the tower. Since v22 a bridge landing after a knock-out prices the route without the knocked-out towers (`S.routeSkip`). Sabotage prices (v18) are one product and one `Math.round`, from counts both devices share (`S.totals.bridgeTries`/`koTries` travel with the defender's result).
- The Wave step's forecast (v22; v19's ghost wave before it) plays the wave on a scratch copy through `offstage`, like `trial`, with its own seed (`seedFor('forecast', round)`): the match, the random stream and the plan are untouched (`test_local.py` compares them before and after). It runs on the attacker's Wave and Sabotage steps, never inside a wave, and the plan carries only the timeline it produced. Auto-sequence, Optimize, Meet and Auto-army only run forecasts and trials.
- v32: the gates are part of the map: `gatesFor(seed)` is a pure function of the map seed both devices derive (`seedFor('map', code, ep)`), `makeTerrain` sets them before it lays terrain, and a map seed that changes in round 1 (the defender's wins) resets them through `startMap`, so nothing new travels; `PROTO` 21 keeps a v31 device out. In a wave units spawn at `ENTRY_R + 0.5` and walk into the keep at `EXIT_R + 0.5` (the sapper's reach checks the same point), so with `fixedGates` a wave plays v31's to the bit, and v32's starter maze with `mazeTwin: 0, mazeHairpin: 0, fixedGates: true` is v31's on all 1,000 maps checked. Everything else in v32 is drawn or heard only: the turrets' drawn angle and recoil (`towerAim`), the units' drawn heading, bank and path (`uview`, which steps the route field with `nextStep` and puts `stepDir` back), the suggestion, the surfaces and the sounds read the wave and never write it; `shotFx`/`launchFx` move only their flash and streak to the drawn barrel, while the shell starts where the simulation says. Nothing is added to the simulation's unit objects.
- v31: Chain's jump count is part of a tower's tuning, so it travels in the grid (its own slot, `PROTO` 20) and `chainFrom` reads it on both devices; `chainOf` turns an old `true` into 3, so 3 jumps plays v30's chain to the bit (54 matches compared). The new tower art only reads the config.

- v29: the art and sound only read the wave. The sim's visual calls (`shotFx`, `launchFx`, `blastFx`, `chainFx`, `deathFx`, `keepFx`, `knockFx`, `bridgeFx`, `dmgNum`, `floatText`, `missPuff`, `shotSfx`, `sfx`) return at once in a hidden wave (`QUIET`), use `Math.random` only, and change nothing the wave reads; the miss branch in `updateTowers` skips `missBeside` in a hidden wave, which only ever drew. `fcAt` gained a heading (`a`) for drawing. The start screen's scene keeps its own lists and never touches `S`, `srand` or `fx`. `test_online.py` and `test_local.py`'s replay and test-run checks pass unchanged.
- v28: whether a move pays a fee (`feeOn`) only matters on the defender's device, whose plan carries what it paid (`mf`, 0 with sabotage off); `PROTO` 19 anyway, so a v27 and a v28 device never play one match on two fee rules.
- v27: interest is settled on both devices in `endRound` from the free coins both hold, so moving its cap changes what both compute: `PROTO` 18 keeps a v26 device out of a v27 match. The move fee's rise only matters on the defender's device, as in v25.
- v25: the move fee's schedule only matters on the defender's device (its plan carries what it paid, `mf`, and the count, `mt`), so a different rise couldn't desync a match; `PROTO` went to 16 anyway, so a v24 and a v25 device never play one match on two fee schedules.
- v24: the sabotage choice changes what both devices compute (units' health, which charges decode), so it's the match's (`S.sabo`), set from the defender's pick: the presence carries it (`sb`) and so does the defender's plan, which the attacker's device adopts in `beginOnlineWave` before the plans are decoded (`adoptSabo`), the way `n` is.
- v23: everything new is settled in `endRound` from numbers both devices already hold: the move fees from the defender's plan (`mf`, decoded in `beginOnlineWave`, like `dFree`), interest from each side's decoded free coins at `prepWave`, the shot-down payout from the decoded sabotage prices (`S.sapList[].cost`) and the wave itself. The fees can't be derived from the seen grid and the grid at lock-in, as the spec suggested: the rate rises with the order of the moves, a tower may be retuned between moves, and a removed tower is gone from the grid, so the defender's device records what it paid and the plan carries it. Which towers move free (`S.freeMv`) only matters on the defender's device. Tower prices are one `Math.pow` each, as before.
- v22: a bridge landing re-routes on the towers firing at that moment: `layBridge` records the knocked-out towers in `S.routeSkip` and `recompute` leaves them out until the wave ends, so a knock-out after it never moves the route (the set is plain cell numbers, the same on both devices). The toughness surcharge is one more multiply before `unitCost`'s single `Math.round`. The move fee is the defender's own business: its coins travel in its plan (`dFree`), the count in its plan (`mt`) and its result (`T.moveTries`).
- v21.1: several resists are flags read on each hit (`resOf`), the same arithmetic as one resist, and travel as a bitmask (`resMask`), so both devices decode the same set. v21: chain jumps pick the nearest unit by `hyp` within `chainReach` of the unit struck before (ties to the earlier unit in `S.units`), brittle is one multiply on the damage that lands, and the frost resist's slow is `frostSlow * 4 / 10` so the v20 values give v20's 0.16 to the bit. The pot (`potFor`/`splitFor`) is computed as `lo + (hi − lo) × …` so the v20 values give v20's payout to the bit; `test_local.py` checks both. The starter maze is a pure function of the map (`starterWalls` runs `mazeSearch` to its end synchronously: its `yield`s are pause points only), so both devices build it from the shared seed and nothing about it travels. The pot meter, the MVPs and the coin count-up only read the wave.
- After any change to wave logic, run `tools/tests/test_online.py`. It checks that both devices end identical, including with one device at 10× speed.
- The computer opponent runs waves out of sight (`trial`) with `QUIET` set. Anything new in the wave code that touches the DOM, redraws the terrain or ends the round must be skipped while `QUIET` is true, the way `leak`, `detonate` and `endRound` do it. Effects pushed to `fx`/`floaters` are trimmed by `trial` itself.
- The replay relies on the same determinism: `test_local.py` checks that a replay ends with exactly the real wave's numbers. A test run (v14) does too: the same build against the same army gives last wave's result exactly.
- The build helpers (hot tiles, the readout, shapes' previews) only read the build and use `flow` on `tmpA`/`tmpB`; they never run inside a wave, and a test run restores `srand` and `uid` like `trial` does. The bridge rule is checked again in `decodeCharges` on both devices from `S.seenGrid` and `S.bridges`, which both devices share, so a plan with an off-route bridge loses it identically on both.

### Online protocol (presence-style, absolute state)

Each side publishes `{v, id, role, h, code, ep, st, n, tm, sb, rd, lk, go, at, plan, res}` (v26: `h` 1 for the host; `role` `'def'`, `'atk'`, `'spec'` for a spectator or `''` for a joiner without a seat; `st` `'lobby'`, `'play'` or, for a spectator watching, `'watch'`; everyone in the room publishes one, and every device reads them all) (`tm`, v23: timed rounds, 1 or 0; `sb`, v24: sabotage on, 1 or 0, also in the defender's plan; the attacker plays the defender's, like `n`). The same object travels over the claude.ai room, the relay or PeerJS. The flow:

1. A player who presses Ready sets `lk` to the round number.
2. Once both sides are locked, each sets `go` and reveals `plan`. Plans stay private until both commit.
3. The wave starts when both have `go === round`.
4. The defender publishes `res` (including the bridges on the map, `br`), and the attacker adopts it if their copy disagrees, so the defender's device is the referee. Its plan also carries the map seed (`ms`). Rock never travels in a grid: it comes from the map on each device.
5. `ep` is the match epoch. A rematch or a rejoin after a reload bumps it, and the other side follows. A device that reloads mid-match can't resume; the lobby offers to restart.
6. v26: the host presses Start once the other seat is taken; the player in it starts on seeing the host's presence go to `play` in round 1. The host's settings (`n`, `tm`, `sb`) are the match's: a joiner takes them in the lobby and publishes them, so the defender's presence and plan carry them whoever hosts, and everything below works as before.
7. `n` is the match length (v19): the lobby pick until the match starts, then the match's. The attacker plays the defender's: `startOnlineMatch` takes it from the defender's presence, `adoptRounds` catches a late change, and the defender's plan carries it too (`n`, checked in `beginOnlineWave` before the plans are decoded, since `decodeCharges` allows no sabotage in the last wave). A rematch keeps it. The planning clock never travels: each device times its own player and presses Ready for them (`lockIn`).

### Single-player (versus computer)

The mode screen has a third card, **Versus computer**. The player picks a side and a level (Easy, Normal or Hard), and the computer takes the other seat. There are no pass-the-device screens.

**Fair play:** the computer knows only what a human in its seat would.
- As attacker: `seenGrid`, and the bridges and terrain on the map (public: both sides see them), and how many bridges and knock-outs it has sent (they price the next ones).
- As defender: its own grid, `seenArmy`, the attacker's coins (the HUD shows those to both players), and how many tower sappers the attacker has sent (the defender saw them march).
- `soloKnows` copies that at the start of the round, before the player does anything, and the computer never reads the live state again that round.

**Propose, test, pick:** principles generate candidate plans, each is played out in a hidden copy of the real wave (`trial`), and the best one is played.
- `trial` swaps in a scratch state and runs the real `waveStep` with `QUIET` set, then restores `S`, `srand`, `uid`, the effects and the route field.
- One trial costs about 6–15 ms on a desktop, and 16–22 ms with the CPU throttled 4×. The defender's round is 60–120 trials at Hard on a terrain map (walled-off variants are tested with one spending style only), about 0.5–0.7 s on a desktop and 2.2–2.9 s throttled 4×; the attacker's is lighter.
- **Attacker** (`thinkAttack`):
  - Army ideas (`AI_ARMIES`: swarm, rush, ghost, plated, bruiser, hulk, brutes screening a swarm, runners with ghosts), each bunched and spread, against the seen defense. Since v19 its Ghost has no dodge: it's the new Ghost preset, 112 HP at 1.9 cells/s.
  - The best few against that defense retuned to counter them (`retuneAgainst`), then with sapper options (v17):
    - a bridge on the seen wall that shortens the route most (`bridgePicks`), only walls with a sapper tile (`bridgeSpots`), within `bridgesPerWave`
    - a knock-out on the tower that covers the route most (`towerPicks`), or the best that fit the value cap together (`capFit`)
    - a bridge plus a knock-out
    - each with its sappers leaving early (20%) or halfway (50%) into the first group of the army (`AI_SAP_T`, `sapT`; Easy only early). The trial waves play the sappers out, so it finds out whether they get there: through the counter-tuned defense too, where moved tower ranges can take a tile off the route.
    - priced by the rules and within the caps (`sapPlan`, from `K.bridgeTries`/`K.koTries`; `capFit` on slider prices); none in round 1 or the last (`K.rounds`, v19)
    - rough ground counts as a lane the defender can't wall: a bridge that opens onto it is valued as one that can't be walled off, and the note says so
  - Coins spent must pay for themselves: they are scored at cost × breach × later rounds that matter, capped by how long the keep would last at this pace. So it spends against strong defenses and late in the match, and not when the army already walks through.
  - A bridge also keeps paying in those later rounds, for a share (`BRIDGE_HOLD`) of what it added this wave: a small share when a wall or two at its ends would take it away again (`bridgeBlock` finds one), a bigger one when it can't be walled off.
  - v16's multi-wave tower charges (and `TOWER_HOLD`) went with v17: a knock-out lasts the wave it lands in.
  - Its note says which wall it sent a sapper to bridge and how much shorter the route gets if it lands, or that a new bridge didn't pay for itself, and when its sappers left.
  - Round 1 is a random opening from `AI_OPENERS` when it has seen nothing; since v21 the starter maze is seen, so round 1 runs the army ideas against it in trial waves like any round (no sabotage in round 1), and its note says so ("I tried N plans against the starter maze…").
  - Shock (v21): when a fair share of the seen defense chains (`readDefense(...).chain` ≥ 30% of tower value), the best ideas are also tried with every unit taking the Shock resist (`aiArmy(..., 'shock')`); the trials decide, and the note says "with shock resist against your chain towers".
- **Defender** (`thinkDefend`): every maze (`AI_MAZES`: 3, 4 or 5 serpentine columns, towers in the cells covering the most route), tower mix (`AI_MIXES`, since v21 with a `chain` mix: chain on every other tower, never with splash; `retuneAgainst` clears chain when it adds splash) and spending style (`AI_SPEND`), against the last army scaled to the attacker's coins now. Then the best few against a panel of armies that could beat them (`threatsFor`). It sells everything and rebuilds, so in round 1 the starter walls are just 5 coins each in its worth (about 450 to build with).
  - It keeps its maze unless another is clearly better (15% margin).
  - **Terrain:** each maze family can sit a column left or right (`MAZE_KEYS`: `four-1`, `three+1`, …). `pickMazes` takes, per family, the shift that forces the longest route on this map with its holes walled off (`mazeInfo`, cached per map and bridge set), so rock in a column serves as a free wall and columns step around rough ground. Rough ground in a column is a hole like a bridge, handled by the same build modes. The note says when it shifted and how many rocks it uses.
  - **Bridges:** nothing can be built on one, so a bridge in a maze's columns leaves a hole. Every maze is tried as it is (towers placed along the route the bridges open, `aiSlots` with the bridges) and, where walls help, with the bridges walled off at one end (`blockBridges`, 5 coins a wall). Its finalists also come with a tower beside each bridge the route crosses (`killCells`, a kill zone). A maze whose columns miss the bridges is a relocation, and new walls can't be bridged for a round. The tests decide which; the note says what it did ("I moved my maze…", "I walled off one end…", "I put a tower beside…", "I built around…"), and `brainPlan` returns it as `info.response` for the sims.
  - **Guard towers (Normal and Hard):** a bridge misses if its wall has become a tower, so each finalist also comes in a guarded version, with a tower on the wall the computer would bridge itself in the attacker's place (`bridgePicks` on `seenGrid`, under the same bridge rules). Its tests decide whether that pays. Hard's extra test sends a bridge sapper for the best allowed wall still standing, its tile taken from the route the attacker saw (`sapPlan` on `seenGrid`), so a new build that moves the route off that tile shows up as a sapper that misses. The note says when it did this.
  - **Dodging knock-outs (v16, Normal and Hard):** targets are tied to the cell (v12), so a tower moved to a cell the attacker saw as a wall is safe for the round.
    - Once the attacker has sent a tower sapper this match (`K.koTries > 0`, public), it expects knock-outs from round 2 to the one before last: on the seen towers it would target itself (`towerPicks` on `seenGrid`, up to two within the value cap), the sappers leaving early in the last army it saw.
    - Each finalist then also comes in a dodged version (`buildDefense(..., avoid)`, `aiSlots` without those cells): the next best cells in the same columns take the towers, and the old cells get walls.
    - Every finalist is judged half on the wave without the charges and half with them (Hard also counts the charged wave toward its worst case). Guarded and kill-zone versions keep the towers of the build they came from, so they take its charge loss instead of a test wave of their own.
    - The note says when it moved towers ("…to a new cell, so a sapper sent for the old spot finds nothing"); `info.expected`/`info.dodged` for the sims.
    - Expecting charges from an attacker who never charges cost Hard keep in the sims (e.g. "always bridge" 348 → 78), so neither level does that.
  - It doesn't use hints, shapes, test runs or auto-tune; those are for the human defender. Nor does the computer attacker use the scrubber (v19; auto-align before it): it sets its timelines directly.
  - Upgrades always go to the weakest tower, so no single tower carries the defense. Since v19 each upgrade is a damage or an accuracy step, whichever adds more expected damage (damage × per-shot accuracy) per coin, priced as the slider is (`aiUpgrade`); with the slider prices, damage nearly always wins until it's maxed.
- **Levels** (`SOLO_LEVELS`):
  - Easy: 3 random ideas from the lighter armies, or 3 tower mixes on its current maze (built around the bridges or walling them off, never relocating), no foresight; picks at random among its top 3; at most one sapper a wave, always leaving early.
  - Normal: everything, averaged with the likely counters; takes the second best 10% of the time.
  - Hard: everything, weighing the worst counter too; the defender also tests the last army with a bridge on its best wall; never blunders. No coin bonus at any level.
- **Variety:** a per-match seeded stream (`SOLO.rng`; tests set `SOLO.seed`).
- **Notes:** after each wave the summary shows "The computer's thinking", which says what it saw, how many plans it tried and what it chose. A note says "What held best" or "got the most through" only when it really played its best plan.

**Timing:** `soloStartRound` starts the thinking. `pumpThink` runs it in slices of about 12 ms between frames, with a 3 s cap (`THINK_MS`) on looking for better plans.
- If the player presses Ready first, `soloReady` shows the `#ov-wait` card ("Keep planning" unlocks).
- The plan uses the online plan format (`{dFree, ts, g}` or `{aFree, a, c}`). `soloBeginWave` applies it with the online decoders.
- So nothing the computer does shows while the player plans, and its plan can't depend on the player's.
- `online()` stays false in solo, so online determinism is untouched. A snapshot restores the player's own phase, and the computer thinks again.
- **Replay and thinking:** both swap `S`. A test wave can run while a replay is open: `trial` restores whatever `S` was, and `endRound` checks `QUIET` before `replay`, so a test wave never ends the replay. `soloBeginWave` closes an open replay before the computer's wave starts.

  - **Auto-maze** (v20.4, the user's "Defender QoL" spec): spends a budget on walls that make the route as long as practical, around whatever is built (it never moves, removes or retunes anything, and never places a tower). Budget per press: `RULES.autoMazeFirst` (60) coins in the first wave, which leaves 240 for four plain towers (220) and a few upgrades; after that `RULES.autoMazeShare` (40%) of the free coins, rounded down to whole walls. The search is the hot tiles taken to their end: each step tries every straight run of new walls along a row or a column that crosses the route (walls, towers and rock inside a run cost nothing; a gate, a bridge or rough ground breaks it; a run of two or more must touch the board's edge or something solid at one end), measured on the pathing units use, and keeps the one that adds the most route per wall; ties go to more route, then more time under fire, then the route that passes a better tower spot (`spotScore`: the most route one default tower could cover from a free cell). It takes about 0.1–0.3 s on a desktop, sliced between frames (the button reads "Building…"); a change to the build meanwhile drops it. Afterwards the row says "Auto-maze built 10 walls for 50 coins · path 15 → 25", and one Undo takes the whole maze back.

  - **Shapes** (`SEGMENTS`, under `RULES`): U-turn, Zigzag, Chokepoint, Corridor, each a 5×5 grid of walls (`#`), towers (`T`) and open cells. 5×5 because a U-turn needs walls on both sides of its turn, which 4×4 can't hold; on a 390 px phone the board is about 25 px a cell with the shapes row open, so a ghost is about 125 px, comfortably bigger than a fingertip. The piece is turned and flipped as it looks on screen (the portrait board is transposed). If the piece would close the route the whole ghost turns red and nothing is placed ("Path must stay open"). If that's more than the free coins it can't be placed, and the message says how many coins are short. Placed pieces are ordinary walls and towers.
  - **Hints** (on by default, all modes, remembered per device in localStorage under `tptd-hints`, read and written inside try/catch): they show the hot tiles and the time under fire. The route preview and path length stay on either way.
  - **Hot tiles**: up to `RULES.hintSetSize` (3) walls chosen greedily: the wall that makes the route longest, then the best with that one in place, and so on. Route length is measured on the pathing units use (`flow` with `dangerField` and the bridge tie-break, then the traced route's steps); only cells on the current route can lengthen it, so only those are tried. Cells that would close the route or that the player can't afford are skipped, ties go to more time under fire, and nothing shows if no wall lengthens the route. Worked out a slice at a time (`RULES.hintSliceMs`) between frames, cached per build (`S.gridVer` and free coins up to the set's price), hidden until ready. They glow with the Wall tool on the Build step. Hovering one previews the whole set (route, length and under-fire change, and a board label "PLACE 3 WALLS · 15 COINS"); one click places the whole set as normal walls (on touch: first tap previews, second places). **The catch:** with hints on, clicking a glowing tile places the set, not one wall; players who want a single wall there can turn hints off. Revisit after playtesting.
  - **Time under fire**: seconds the average unit spends in range of at least one working tower: walking the route, each step whose midpoint is in some tower's range (each tower's own range) counts one cell at the average speed, the mean of `US.speed.steps` (`avgSpeed`, derived, so it follows the slider table). Shown next to the path length ("Path 38 · Under fire 14.2s"); hovering a wall, tower, shape or hot set shows the change ("Under fire 14.2s → 17.6s (+3.4s)"), and the arrow only appears when the number changes. Hidden with hints off.

Best gain per coin first, until no step that adds anything fits; then the cheapest step anywhere, until nothing fits. It never moves, adds or removes anything, lowers a slider, touches fire rate (free, and the player's call against armor) or retunes an offline tower. Afterwards the row reads "Spent X coins on N upgrades, Y left", and Undo (the shared one since v20.4) restores every tower's settings and the coins. The computer defender keeps its own tuning.

Bridges and terrain on the map count; last wave's sappers don't (their targets were picked on an older defense), and the result card says "Sabotage not included." when last wave had any. Nothing changes in the match. - **Terrain** (v11): every match gets its own map from a seed (`startMap`; online, from the match code and epoch, so both devices agree; a rematch gets a new one).
  - **Rough ground** (`roughShare = 0.05`, 9 tiles): units cross it like open ground; nothing can be built on it, and it can't be bridged (it's already open).
  - **Rock** (`rockShare = 0.05`, 9 tiles): blocks units like a wall the defender gets for free; can't be removed, built on or bridged. The attacker sees it from round 1, but it doesn't unlock charges.
  - Placement: the board is split into 3×3 zones and each gets the mean share of each kind, give or take one (0–2 tiles at 5%), in clusters of 1–3 tiles that never touch another cluster. The entry, the keep and the cells around them stay clear.
  - Validation: a route must exist, and the computer's mazes must still force at least `MAP_ROOM` (80%) of the route they force on an empty board. Otherwise it rerolls (`MAP_TRIES` 12), then halves the tiles, then plays without terrain. Over 1,000 seeds: 82% pass first time, none needed the fallback, and the worst map keeps 82% of the maze route.
- **Board and route:** 16×11 grid, entry at the left middle, keep at the right middle. Building may never close the route. Units follow the cheapest route: each cell costs 10, plus `RULES.danger = 5` for every tower that can hit it. So units steer around tower range when the detour is short, and walls are what force them through fire. A tower knocked out mid-wave still counts (v19, at the user's request: "path shouldn't change if towers are disabled mid waves"), so a knock-out silences a tower without moving the route; only a bridge going down re-routes units mid-wave, and since v22 that re-route counts only the towers firing then.
- **Rounds:** 8 by default, 4–12 picked at the start (see Match length). Each round the defender builds while the attacker plans, in secret. The wave then plays out with everything revealed.
- **Hidden information:**
  - The attacker sees the defense as it stood after the last wave (`seenGrid`).
  - The defender sees the last army sent (`seenArmy`).
  - Sabotage only works on things the attacker saw. Rebuilding on the same cell doesn't make something "new" (`seenAs`), which closed a loophole. So knock-outs are tied to the spot: a tower rebuilt where the attacker saw one still goes offline, a tower moved to a new cell is safe for a round (the user confirmed this in v12; no sapper follows a moved tower), and a spot left empty or walled makes it miss.
  - Bridges already on the map are public.
- **Economy:** both start with 300 coins. The keep has 125 points per wave plus 25 for every wave short of 8 (1,000 over 8); the attacker wins at 0, the defender by surviving every round. Each round adds a 200-coin pot (growing since v21). Since v23 each wave also pays interest to both sides (15% of the coins left unspent when it started; up to 150 coins counted until v26, at most 150 paid since v27), the defender's move fees to the attacker, and half a shot-down sapper's price to the defender, all when it ends.
  - Army strength X is the cost of the units sent. Through-strength Y is cost × health fraction left, for each unit that reaches the keep. Breach = Y/X, and the keep loses Y.
  - Defender's pot share = lo + (hi − lo) × (breach ÷ 50%), clamped to lo–hi, of the round's pot; since v21 both grow with the round (see "What v21 changed"; v20 was 30–70% of 200 every round). If no army is sent, the split is 50/50.
  - Everything is fully refundable: towers at their tuning plus the highest build fee paid (v12, see Towers), walls 5 (a bridged wall is refunded automatically), units at cost. Exception: sabotage coins are spent when the wave starts, landed or not. Sappers are not army strength: they don't count in X, never damage the keep, and one shot down isn't counted as a kill.
- **Towers:** one tower type, tuned with sliders.
  - Price: 40 × range multiplier (in proportion since v23, by its square before) × damage multiplier (^1.4 since v23, ^1.15 before) × accuracy × splash × frost 1.35 × poison 1.45 × chain 1.5, plus a build fee of 10 × towers owned. Fire rate is free.
  - **Accuracy** (v19, the user's "Tower Accuracy & Wave Scrubber" spec): the Accuracy slider (after Damage, before Fire rate) is 80/87/94/100% at ×1/1.15/1.35/1.6, so a Basic tower still costs 40 and 64 at the top step; the presets keep 80%. Each shot's chance is the slider plus the fire rate's offset, `RULES.rateAcc` = −8/−5/−2/0/+3/+6 for 4/2.5/1.6/1/0.6/0.4 shots/s, clamped to 0–100% (`shotAcc`): 100% on every shot needs the top step at 1/s or slower, or 94% at 0.4/s; rapid fire never gets there. Fire rate stays free: heavy shots now hit more often as well as beating armor; rapid fire keeps frost and poison going and wastes less on units about to die.
  - **A shot** (v19): every shot fired rolls once on `srand()`, always. Below the shot's accuracy it hits and everything works as before (armor, the 20% floor, splash, effects). A miss from a single-target tower does nothing: no damage, no effects. A missed splash shot still goes off where its target is: the target takes nothing, every other unit in the blast takes its usual splash and the tower's effects. Chain jumps only from a hit, never roll, and always land at half damage. Sappers are shot like any unit, so they gain from misses too. On the board a miss is a grey tracer to a point `missOffset` (0.3) cells beside the target and a small grey puff, with no damage number (a splash miss: the blast where the target is, the puff beside it).
  - The slider labels read "87% · 82% per shot at this fire rate" and "2.5 per second · 7.2 per hit · 82% accurate"; a tower's card adds "Accuracy 82% per shot" and "Expected damage 18 per second · about 14 after misses", and its price line the accuracy multiplier.
  - Fees count towers, not which one goes (v12): selling any tower gives back its tuning plus the highest fee paid, and the tower that paid that takes over the sold one's smaller fee (`sellTower`). So moving a tower and rebuilding it with the same tuning leaves the coins exactly where they were, and the fees between n towers are always 0, 10, …, 10 × (n − 1).
  - Presets: Basic, Cannon (splash), Sniper, Frost. Offline towers still block their cell and can't be removed or retuned (since v17 a knock-out only lasts the wave, so the defender never plans around one).
- **Walls:** 5 coins each.
- **Units:** up to 4 permanent designs. Units that die come back next wave.
  - Price: 15 × health/150 × speed/1.3 × armor × regen × resist.
  - **Big-unit surcharge** (v6): above `RULES.bigUnit = 60` coins, price = 60 × (raw ÷ 60)^1.5.
  - **Toughness surcharge** (v22, after it): toughness T = (HP ÷ 150) × (speed ÷ 1.3); the price is multiplied by 1 + 0.25 × max(0, T − 2.5) (`toughK`, `toughT0`). A fully maxed unit (900 HP, 2.8 cells/s, armor 3, T 12.9) costs 3,823 (1,060 in v21.1).

No dodge since v19 (it was 10/20/30% of direct shots in v15–v18, 20/35/50% before): tower accuracy took its place as the source of misses. The Ghost preset is now 112 HP at 1.9 cells/s and nothing else (16 coins; it was 19 with 20% dodge).
  - **Regeneration:** 1/2/3% of max health per second, from the moment the unit is hurt, even under fire (v23, `regenDelay` 0; v6-v22.2 waited `regenDelay = 3` s after the last direct hit), at most `regenCap` (8 since v23, 10 before) % × level of max health per wave.
  - Resists: 60% less splash, 60% less poison, 60% weaker frost (and never brittle, v21), 60% less chain (Shock, v21). Since v21.1 a design takes any number of them, each ×1.3 on the price (all four ×2.86, before the big-unit surcharge).

Meet shifts the group so the middle of its time in the zone matches the other's, from the forecast, correcting twice; the note says "Brutes now pass through Z3 with the Knock-out sapper", or "Closest possible: …", or "… never reach Z3"; the clock jumps there and the zone is picked.
  - **Zones** are the seen towers whose ranges cover overlapping or touching stretches of the route, numbered Z1, Z2… in the order the forecast's front unit enters them, rebuilt when the sabotage plan changes. Tags from the towers: Splash ✸, Chain ϟ, Heavy ■ (single shots of 25 or more), Basic •, Frost ✻, Poison ☁, each with a threat and a counter. On the map their route tiles glow red-orange with a "Z3 ✕6" tag; a picked zone draws its towers' range rings. The strip has a chip per zone: symbols, stars (losses ÷ army units that entered it: 3★ at 5% or less, 2★ 20%, 1★ 40%), ✕ (counting up while playing), the change since the plan before the last change ("6→0 ▲"), LOCKED. A chip (or a zone's tile or tower on the map) picks the zone and jumps the clock to its most crowded moment. The zone card: its title and stars, THREAT and BEAT IT, and LAST RUN · EVERY ZONE (deaths by design and the damage each zone's towers dealt), with Optimize Zk (Auto-sequence scored on the fewest losses there, then breach: −coins lost there + 5 × breach) and Lock Zk (its losses now are a cap Auto-sequence and Optimize never raise; your own changes can).
  - **Auto-sequence** improves the plan in up to 4 steps, each the best of the named moves (a group 3 s earlier or later, tighter (span × 0.5), looser (× 1.7, at least 3 s), in pulses, leading the wave, a sapper inside a group) if it gets more through; locks are hard limits. It runs a forecast at a time between frames ("Working it out…"); any edit cancels it. The change log says "Auto-sequenced" or "Optimized Z3 · kept Z1 locked", every zone whose losses changed ("Z1 ✕2→0 ▲ · Z4 ✕6→8 ▼"), "through 41.2% → 50.6%", and the moves numbered, repeats merged.
  - **Undo** (its button by RELEASES, or Ctrl+Z) takes back each change on the step, one press each, Auto-sequence, Optimize, Meet, Split, Merge, Link and presets included. Runs, locks and Undo belong to the round's planning.
  - **Data:** groups are UI only. A design's release times stay in one list in order (`tl.pos`, with `tl.s`/`tl.e` its first and last), each group a list of its units' places in it, so the plan online is exactly as before. Groups last the round, and the next one when the design's count is unchanged. The computer attacker and the sim bots set timelines directly.
- **Sappers** (v17, from the user's "Sappers & Sabotage Rework" spec): all sabotage marches with the army. Each planned bridge or knock-out is carried by a sapper and only happens if the sapper gets there; shot down, its coins are lost with it.
  - Sappers walk the route like any unit and towers shoot them like any unit (no targeting modes yet: towers stay blind to them, which means a sapper behind other units is shielded, since towers shoot whoever is nearest the keep). Fixed stats in `RULES.sapper` (slider indexes): 112 HP (`hp: 2`, chosen with the sims, see "Measured on v17") at 1.6 cells/s (`speed: 4`), armor 1 since v23 (3 a hit; none before), no regen or resist. They cost nothing beyond the sabotage price (the user's answer to the spec's open question).
  - Drawn as a dark diamond rimmed in the payload's colour (orange deck for a bridge, violet bolt for a knock-out), grey and dashed once its sabotage has failed, so replays show which unit did what. During the wave the target of each sapper still on its way stays marked: the planned bridge outline with the sapper's tile, or a violet ring on the tower with its range drawn faintly.
  - A sapper fires the instant it arrives (no planting delay) and is used up. One that has failed (its tile is no longer on the route ahead, the route never comes within the tower's range, the tower is gone) walks on and does nothing at the keep. Checked at spawn and after every step to a new cell, on the route field as it is then; once failed it stays failed.
  - The defender loses nothing for changing the route to make a sapper miss.
  - The summary shows "Sappers landed 1 / 2" (and how many were shot down); the end screen totals it. That success rate is the spec's playtest metric (healthy 40–60%).
- **Sabotage** (the attacker's step 2) shows two cards since v12, **Bridge a wall** and **Knock out a tower**, each with what it does and its price (the knock-out card the range over the towers that still fit). Picking one frames its targets on the map; tapping a wall or a tower works whichever card is picked, and picks that card. A tower opens its card with "Send a sapper · N coins". Each tower that can still be targeted shows its knock-out price on the map (not in bridge mode); towers that no longer fit the cap dim.
- **Knock-out cap bar** (v18): its own row under the two cards, and again in a tapped tower's card, whenever a tower was seen. Left: "Knock-out cap: 132 of 200"; right: what this round's knock-outs cost ("139 coins"). The fill is the targets' slider prices in violet; a tapped, unplanted tower adds a lighter extension, red if it would pass the cap ("Over the cap by 34", and Send is disabled). A first target worth more than the cap fills the bar: "One tower always fits", and nothing else can be added. When no untargeted tower fits any more: "Cap reached".
- **Prices** (v18, the user's "Sabotage Pricing & Helpers" spec): bridges and knock-outs have their own counters and rise in a straight line. Bridge = round(`bridgeBase` × (1 + `bridgeRise` × b)) = 25 × (1 + 0.25 b): 25, 31, 38, 44, 50. Knock-out = round((`towerOffBase` + `towerOffShare` × C) × (1 + `towerOffRise` × t)) = (15 + 0.2 C) × (1 + 0.25 t), where C is the tower's slider price as seen (`towerCost(cfg)`, no build fee, so identical towers cost the same): a Basic tower (C 40) 23, 29, 35, 40, 46; a Sniper (102) 35, 44, 53, 62, 71. b and t count the attempts of that kind in earlier waves, landed or not (`S.totals.bridgeTries`/`koTries`, counted in `prepWave`, so they travel with the defender's result online). Sabotage planned in the same round doesn't raise each other's price, so plan order doesn't matter. `decodeCharges` prices everything by the rules. (v17 had one shared counter compounding 1.25× per attempt, on the tower's value with its fee.)
- **Bridges** (replaced wall charges in v10; planned in the attacker's step 2, Sabotage):
  - Placed on a wall the attacker saw, hidden like all sabotage (so never on rock or rough ground). Its sapper has to reach **its tile**: the first tile of the route as the attacker saw it beside the wall (`anchorOf`, never a bridge), marked on the board with a short line to the wall. The moment it arrives the bridge goes down: the wall's 5 coins go back to the defender and the cell is open ground for the rest of the match. If the defender changed the route so it no longer passes that tile, the bridge misses. A wall behind the wall: the bridge still goes down but leads nowhere.
  - Drawn as a square deck with no direction (v11); neighbouring bridges join into one deck, with rails only on the outer edges (`drawBridgeShape`).
  - Nobody can build on a bridge. Taking the wall down first changes nothing as long as the route still passes the sapper's tile: the bridge lands on the empty cell. A tower built on that cell by then stops it ("BRIDGE MISSED"; the tower is new), and then the bridge's coins are lost.
  - Units treat it as any open cell; of two equally cheap routes they take the one over the bridge (the `FK` tie-break). Since v22 a bridge landing mid-wave re-routes everyone counting only the towers firing at that moment (Part A), and the Sabotage step warns when the forecast shows nobody taking it. Towers next to it cover it. The defender may wall off either end, as long as a route stays open.
  - Price: see Prices (v10–v16 it was 50 + 20 × bridges on the map). None in the first wave (nothing seen) or the final wave. There is no move penalty or rebuild fee for the defender: the bet is that bridges eat map space, so free rebuilding gets harder late in the match.
  - **One bridge per wave** (`RULES.bridgesPerWave = 1`; it was `bridgeCap` before v14, same value). Once one is planned the Bridge card greys out with "One bridge per wave."
  - **A bridge must touch the route** (v14): at least one of the four cells beside it must be on the route as the attacker sees it (`seenPath()`, the seen defense with the bridges on the map). So a bridge is a shortcut on the existing route, not an escape hatch. **Chaining** (v17): a wall beside a bridge that is joined to the route counts too, and its sapper stands on the route beside that bridge (in code, bridge tiles count as connected to the route when checking a wall). So a thick wall can be broken through one layer a wave, which gives the defender time to answer. Only such walls are marked in Sabotage; another wall shows the usual red cross and "Bridges must connect to the current path." `decodeCharges` drops an off-route bridge from any plan, so online, solo and the sim bots obey it too.
- **Knock-out** (v17; the tower charge before): its sapper stops the tower shooting for the rest of the wave the moment it comes within the tower's range (range, not being hit, is the trigger: a slow tower might never hit it). The tower comes back next round. The 1/2/3-wave choice of v3–v16 is gone: the spec prices one knock-out, and it acts mid-wave. Since v19 it doesn't move the route: units keep walking the way they chose with the tower firing (v17–v18 re-routed everyone the moment it went down, often into the gap it had guarded). The attacker's projected route and the scrubber's ghost wave only change for bridges.
  - **Knock-out cap:** the towers targeted in one wave can add up to at most `towerCapShare` (half) of the slider prices C of all the towers the attacker saw, the same measure as the price (`towerCap`, `capRoom`; v17 counted fees too); one tower can always be targeted, however much it's worth. It replaced v4's cap of half the towers.
  - No sabotage in the first wave (nothing seen yet) or the final wave.

## Balance: history, current state, open questions

**History, so you don't undo a fix by accident:**

- **v1:** pass-and-play prototype with walls, towers and explosive charges.
- **v2:** one currency and the breach-split pot. One customisable tower type. A permanent army with a unit designer. Stacked upgrades cost more than separate ones (multiplicative pricing).
- **v3:** the user found towers dominated.
  - Tower charges now knock towers offline. Players felt 40 coins was too much for one wave, so they're 25/55/90.
  - The wave timeline designer was added, and unit prices were lowered.
  - Tower build fees rise with each tower owned, so walls matter by mid-game.
- **v4:**
  - Units steer around tower range. Previously, equal-length routes hugged towers, and towers on open ground were as good as mazes.
  - Wall charge 30 → 40.
  - Cap on offline towers added.
- **v5:**
  - The user reported attackers could never win without knocking out every tower. Full-match simulations agreed: 0% attacker wins against a defender that retunes its towers. The cause was that tower damage potential dwarfed unit health, and a maze multiplies every tower.
  - Unit health ×2.5 at the same prices.
  - No charges in the final wave, at the user's request. Their reasoning: coins spent on charges in wave 7 aren't in the army for wave 8.
  - The rebuild-on-the-same-cell loophole closed.
  - 1×/5×/10× wave speed added.
- **v6:**
  - The user reported attackers always win with charges, and that one unit with maxed health, speed and armor couldn't be stopped. Simulations confirmed both.
  - Wall charge 40 → 60.
  - Big-unit surcharge added.
  - The design scan then found regeneration was the worst offender. A unit healing 6% per second fully recovered between towers, and about two-thirds of high-health regen designs beat every defense. Regen was reworked to the rules above.

## Tools (in `tools/`)

The tools load the real `index.html` in headless Chromium through Playwright, so they always test the current rules. Nothing in the shipped game changes. They write a modified copy (with internals exposed and the AI players attached) into `tools/.build/`, which is gitignored.

**Setup (once per machine):**
```
pip install -r tools/requirements.txt && python -m playwright install chromium
npm install                      # repo root: ws, for server.js and the relay tests
(cd tools/tests && npm install)  # peer + peerjs, only for the online test suite
```

**Simulators (`tools/sim/`):**

- `match.py` (v24: `--rules '{"sabotage": false}'` plays with sabotage off, and `--first N` starts at seed N, to add seeds to an earlier run): full 8-round games between the defender bot (`counter` or `basic`, or the game's own computer defender `game:easy|normal|hard`) and 18 attacker strategies (swarm, rush, maxed tanks, regen bruisers, bridges, tower charges and more; `--attackers computer` runs the game's own computer attacker instead). Prints attacker wins, keep left, breach, the round the keep fell, bridges laid and towers knocked out per match, how many sappers landed (and how many were shot down; v17, the spec's playtest metric, with a total at the end), and how the defender dealt with the bridges on the map, counted per round: `relocate` (moved its maze off them), `clear` (its maze already misses them), `block` (walled off an end), `kill` (tower beside one), `around` (left the hole and built around it). `--rules '{...}'` overrides any `RULES` key or slider table, `--opts '{...}'` merges into every attacker (e.g. `{"charges": "bridges", "chargeShare": 1}` makes every strategy bridge whenever it can), and `--log` prints round-by-round; `--out file.jsonl` keeps every match with its log for `compare.py` (v19). For example:
  `python tools/sim/match.py --defenders counter --rules '{"bridgeBase": 35, "bridgeRise": 0.4}'`
  `python tools/sim/match.py --attackers computer --rules '{"sapper": {"hp": 2, "speed": 4}}'` (sappers with other stats)
  `python tools/sim/match.py --attackers computer --rules '{"bridgesPerWave": 0}'` (the computer attacker without bridges)
  - The four bridge strategies (`counter + bridges`, `counter + tower charges`, `counter + bridges & towers`, `alternate + bridges & towers`) take the slots of v9's wall-charge strategies with the same armies and budgets, so v9 and v10 runs line up. `always bridge` is `counter-picks` that lays a bridge whenever one shortens the route. Since v17 their sabotage goes by sapper (the game's `sapPlan`), leaving early in the first group (`--opts '{"sapTime": "mid"}'` sends them halfway in); knock-outs go on the most valuable towers within the value cap.
  - The `counter` bot walls off the ends of bridges and rough ground that cut its maze (the game's `blockBridges`) but never moves its maze; `basic` leaves them open. Both skip rock (it's already a wall) and never build on rough ground. The game's defenders also relocate and shift their mazes.

## Checklist when changing rules or UI

1. Change the number in `RULES` (or `SEGMENTS`) or the slider tables. Don't hardcode it elsewhere. (A tower's kind reads `TS.rate` and `splash` through `towerKind`; if the fire rate steps change, check the kinds still split where they should.)
2. Update the text the player reads:
   - the info overlay: "How to play", "Core mechanics", the counter table and the rules table under "What this demo is"
   - slider hints in `TS`/`US`
   - the sabotage notes and cards in the attacker panel
3. Bump the version label in the info tagline and the colophon (`prototype vN`).
4. Run the four test suites. For balance changes, run `match.py` before and after, plus `scan.py` if unit pricing or abilities changed.
5. Keep it one self-contained file. The only `localStorage` is per device: the Hints switch (`tptd-hints`), whether Remove & Inspect's one-line tip has shown (`tptd-inspect-tip`, v20.4) and the sound switch (`tptd-sound`, v29), all read and written inside try/catch so the page works without it (v20.2's `tptd-wave-guide` went with the How to use card in v22). Check it at phone width (390 px portrait).
