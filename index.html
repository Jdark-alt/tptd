Two Player Tower Defence · v32

How to play

DefenderBuild walls and towers. Hold the keep.
AttackerBuild an army. Break the keep.
Keep1,000 points. Whatever gets through comes off it.
  1. 1
    Plan in secret. The defender builds a maze with towers along it. The attacker picks units and when they set off.
  2. 2
    Watch the wave. Both plans are revealed and the army marches on the keep.
  3. 3
    Collect coins. Each wave pays both sides. Whoever did worse gets more, then you plan the next wave.

The attacker wins by breaking the keep. The defender wins by holding it to the last wave.

A round

  1. Defender, in secret. Every match starts with a free starter maze: ordinary walls you can keep, change or sell for 5 each. Walls bend the route, towers shoot it. Tap a tower to tune it. From round 2, Test plays last wave’s army against your build. Lock in.
  2. Attacker, in secret. Army: pick units. Sabotage, from round 2: walls to bridge and towers to knock out, each carried by a sapper. Wave: time units and sappers, with a forecast of the wave against the defense you saw. Lock in from any step.
  3. The wave. Everything is revealed and plays out; the pot meter in the bar shows how the pot would split if the wave ended now. Tap a unit or tower to inspect it, and replay the wave from the summary.
  4. The split. Each round adds a pot, and the side that got the worse of the wave gets the bigger share: the more gets through, the more the defender takes. Both sides also earn interest: 15% of the coins left unspent when the wave starts, at most 150 a wave (reached with 1,000 saved). The attacker gets the defender’s move fees, and the defender the price of every sapper it shoots down.

Pick how many waves the match lasts, whether there’s sabotage, and whether rounds are timed on the start screen (online, the host’s picks count). With sabotage off there are no bridges or knock-outs: the army has to break the defense on its own, so units get more health, at the same price, and moving towers costs nothing. With timed rounds each side has 5 minutes to plan each round, and when the clock runs out the plan goes as it stands; Untimed has no clock.

Online. One player hosts a room and shares its code (or the invite link); a friend joins and sits in the free seat, and the host starts the match. The host’s settings are the match’s. Anyone with the watch link can watch, before or during a match: up to 8 watchers. They see what the attacker sees between waves, and each wave once both sides have locked in, never a plan before. The eye in the top bar shows how many are watching and copies the link.

Build

Tools. Wall (W), Tower (T), Remove & Inspect (R), Shapes (S). Tap to build and drag to draw walls; on touch screens a tower takes a second tap to place. A tower placed on a wall replaces it, and the wall’s 5 coins come back. Drag a tower to move it, or onto a wall or another tower to swap them. The chips above the tools are your towers: tap one to open its card.

Shortcuts. W, T, R and S pick the tools; with Shapes picked, R turns the piece and F flips it. Delete or Backspace removes the selected tower (Undo brings it back) or deletes the open design. Escape is Done. Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) undoes. M turns the sound on or off (the speaker in the top bar too). On the Wave step the space bar plays the forecast and Ctrl+Z undoes its last change. None of them work while you type in a field.

Auto-maze spends up to 60 coins in the first wave (40% of your free coins after that) on walls that make the route longer, around whatever you’ve built, and, when it can, on a hairpin in the corner by a gate: a short wall the route has to pass on both sides, there and back, so one tower on it covers the route twice. Auto-tune spends your free coins on upgrades to the towers you’ve placed. Undo takes back your last change this round, coins and tuning included, until the wave starts; the starter maze is where a round begins, so Undo never takes it away.

Remove & Inspect: hover a wall (on touch, hold it) and a red line shows where units would go if it were bridged. A click or tap takes a wall or tower back.

Move fee. Moving a tower the attacker saw costs a fee: 10% of its tuning as it is now for your first relocation in the match, then 1% more for each one after, whichever tower it is (10%, 11%, 12%…), at most 40%. Removing a seen tower refunds its value minus the same fee, so dragging and rebuilding cost the same; swapping two seen towers is two relocations. Free: round 1, towers placed this round, putting a tower back on its seen cell this round (its fee comes back), and the round after a sabotage lands: every tower after a bridge went down, the knocked-out towers after a knock-out. The tower card shows the next one (“Next move: 11%”), and the drag label its price. The fees go to the attacker: what you paid this round, less what came back, is theirs when the wave ends. With sabotage off there is no move fee: towers move free all match.

Copy tuning on a tower’s card: new towers you place get that tuning (priced as usual) until you clear it with × on the Tower tool.

Hints (the switch beside the title): glowing tiles mark the walls that would lengthen the route most, and a tap on one places the whole set; the readout shows how long the average unit spends under fire. Early in a round, while you can afford a tower, a faint outline may mark the one spot where a tower would cover the most route (each pass counts); it fades by itself, and goes once you place a tower.

On the board. Units come in at the orange gate on the left and head for the gold keep on the right; each match puts them on rows of their own, one in each half of the board. Bright walls are new this round: the attacker hasn’t seen them and can’t bridge them. Dotted ground is rough: units cross it, nothing can be built on it. Hatched slabs are rock: a free wall. An orange bridge stays open for good: wall off an end, or cover it with towers. Small dots on a tower are its effects: frost, poison, chain.

Coins. Your free coins are the big number under the title; the bar beside them splits your worth into free coins, towers and walls (hover or hold it for the numbers). The top bar keeps the round, the keep, the clock (it grows and turns red for the last 30 seconds) and the pot.

Towers

Tap a tower to open its card under your tools: Wall, Remove and the rest stay a click away, no Done needed.

One tower type. Its tuning starts at 40 coins and every slider multiplies the price, so stacking upgrades on one tower costs more than spreading them. Fire rate is free. Each tower also pays a build fee of 10 for every tower you already own. Removing a tower refunds its tuning and the top fee. Each slider shows a short value and what one more step costs; hold or hover it for the detail.

40 × (range ÷ 2.5) × (damage ÷ 18)^1.4 × accuracy × splash × frost × poison × chain + fee

The default tower (2.5 cells, 18 a second) is ×1 on both, so it still costs 40. A 1.5-cell tower is ×0.6; 75 damage a second is ×7.4. Splash is priced by its blast (×1.5 / ×1.9 / ×2.4) and Chain by its jumps (×1.2 / ×1.35 / ×1.5).

  • Range: priced in proportion to its reach: 5 cells costs twice what 2.5 does, 1.5 cells 0.6 of it. A short-range tower is cheaper, but not by much more than its reach.
  • Damage: total damage per second; fire rate decides how it’s split into shots. Each step costs more than the last, so piling damage on one tower costs more than spreading it over several.
  • Accuracy. Every tower starts at 80%. The slider raises it to 87, 94 or 100% (×1.15, ×1.35, ×1.6), and fire rate shifts each shot: heavy shots hit more often, rapid fire less.
  • Fire rate: free; its slider runs from slow, heavy shots on the left to rapid fire on the right. Heavy shots hit more often and punch through armor; rapid fire keeps frost and poison going.
  • Splash (a chip; ×1.5 / ×1.9 / ×2.4 for a 0.5, 0.9 or 1.3-cell blast): hits everything near the impact; a miss still hits the units around its target. Beats bunched waves and swarms. Checking it opens the smallest blast; its slider makes it bigger.
  • Frost (×1.35): slows each unit it hits by 50% for 2.5 s. A slowed unit is brittle: every tower’s hits on it land 20% harder (not the hit that slows it, and not poison ticks).
  • Poison (×1.45): adds 60% more damage over 3 s, ignoring armor.
  • Chain (a chip; ×1.2 / ×1.35 / ×1.5 for 1, 2 or 3 jumps): each hit jumps up to 3 times, 2.5 cells from unit to unit, for 70%, 50% and 35% of the hit, ignoring armor; never the same unit twice. Checking it gives all three jumps; its slider takes fewer for less. Chain and Splash don’t combine: checking one unchecks the other.
Shots per second
Accuracy

Each shot rolls once. A hit works as always: armor, splash, effects. A miss does nothing and applies no effects, but a splash miss still blasts the units around its target, just not the target. Chain only jumps from a hit, and its jumps always land.

Reading a tower. Every setting shows, in the same place on every tower, for both players. The outline is how it attacks: an octagon shoots (rapid fire a Gatling’s spinning cluster of barrels, slow heavy shots a Railgun’s heavy rails with capacitor blocks, anything else a Blaster’s shrouded barrel), a round dish is a Mortar (splash: a ring on the dish for each step), a three-cornered pylon a Tesla (chain: a node lit in a corner for each jump). What points at the target reaches as far as it is long; the lights along the foot and the turret’s size are its damage (a ring of light at the top step); one to three chevrons on a dish or coil its fire rate; a scope its accuracy. Frost turns its whole base a cold steel blue, with ice growing from it; Venom (poison) fills its turret with dark, glowing green; a tower with both has both.

Army

Up to four permanent designs; units that die come back next wave. Tap a design to open it in place: its sliders, resists, name and colour. − and + sell or buy one unit; Max buys as many as your coins allow, 0 sells them all. Health, speed, armor, regeneration and resistance multiply the price; above 60 coins a surcharge applies, so one giant unit costs more than its parts. Tough units cost more too: above a toughness of 2.5 (health × speed), each point adds 25% to the price.

15 × health × speed × armor × regen × each resist, past 60 coins 60 × (price ÷ 60)^1.5, then × (1 + 0.25 × max(0, toughness − 2.5))

Toughness = (HP ÷ 150) × (speed ÷ 1.3). A Brute is 1.7, a 500 HP unit at 1.9 cells/s is 4.9.

  • Health: price grows in line with it. Speed: fast units spend less time in range, so price grows with it too.
  • Armor: every hit loses 3, 6 or 10, but at least 20% still lands. Beats rapid fire, weak to heavy shots; chain jumps ignore it.
  • Regeneration: heals 1, 2 or 3% of its health a second from the moment it’s hurt, even under fire, up to 10% a wave per 1%. Beats poison and chip damage; heavy, focused fire kills it before it heals much.
  • Resists (×1.3 each; take as many as you like, the prices multiply): Splash takes 60% less splash damage; Poison 60% less poison; Frost is slowed 60% less and is never brittle; Shock takes 60% less chain damage. None clears them.

Reading a unit. Its shape is its speed. Slow designs (1.1 cells/s and under) are heavy plates with a slow pulse: a three-lobed Bastion, an orb behind a crescent shield, a pointed Lens. At mid speed they’re sleek hulls: an Ogive, an Arrowhead, a swept Dart. Fast ones (2.3 and up) are blades split by lines of light, trailing jet streaks: a Splitwing and a Talon. The faster, the longer the trail and the higher it hovers; the bigger, the more health. Steel brackets round it are armor (the front, then the sides, then all round), a green cross at its heart regeneration, and glowing plates orbiting it its resists, in their colours.

Wave plan

Play. ▶ PLAY WAVE runs a forecast of your wave on the defense you saw, towers firing, misses, frost, chain, knock-outs and bridges included, at 3× (the space bar too). Units fade as they’re hurt; a ✕ marks where one fell. Each press saves a run; tap a run to see its losses and restore it. The forecast is your plan against the defense you saw: the defender may have changed it since.

Releases. A row per group on a 20-second track, the gate on the right: a bar nearer the gate leaves sooner. Drag the bar to move the group, its ends to stretch or squeeze it, a dot to move one unit. A sapper is a single ◆. Split a group (pick how many leave first; hovering Split marks the cut) and each part, A and B, moves on its own; Merge joins them again; Link ties groups of different designs at the same speed so they move as one. Meet lines a group (or a sapper) up with another in a zone, or at cells you pick on the map (⌖: tap a cell, or drag along the route for a stretch of it). Adding or selling units on the Army step keeps your timing: new ones join the group that leaves last.

Zones are where the danger is: towers whose ranges overlap on the route. Each shows what it does, how to beat it, its stars (how few you lose there) and what died there last run. Optimize works on one zone; Lock keeps a zone’s losses from going up when Auto-sequence or Optimize work on another. Auto-sequence tries one small change at a time and keeps the best, up to four; a careful plan can beat it.

Route and terrain

Units take the cheapest route: a step per cell, plus half a step for every tower that can hit it. They walk around tower range when the detour is short, so walls are how you force them through fire. A tower knocked out mid-wave still counts, so a knock-out never changes the route. The dashed line always shows the route.

Every match gets a new map. Rough ground (dotted, about 5%) is open to units but nothing can be built on it. Rock (hatched, about 5%) blocks like a free wall and can’t be removed, built on or bridged. The starter maze is built on each map the same way on both devices: the walls 3 presses of Auto-maze would build on the empty board.

Sabotage

Each target gets a sapper that marches with the army. It walks the route like any unit, towers shoot it like any unit, and it only does its job if it gets there. It does nothing at the keep. Time each sapper on the wave plan; units walking ahead of it draw the fire.

Sappers have 112 HP and light armor: every hit loses 3 (at least 20% still lands), so a weak tower at the front can’t pick them off cheaply. Shot down, its coins are lost and 50% of its price goes to the defender when the wave ends. If its target has moved (the tower to another cell, or the route off the bridge’s tile), it misses and its coins are spent: nobody gets them, since the defender already paid you the move fee.

Bridge. Goes on a wall you’ve seen beside the route you saw, or beside one of your bridges. Its sapper lays it on reaching the marked tile beside the wall: the wall’s coins go back to the defender and the cell is open ground for the rest of the match; nobody can build there again. If the route no longer passes that tile, or a tower stands where the wall was, it misses. When it lands everyone, sappers included, takes the cheapest route counting only the towers firing then, so a tower you’ve already knocked out doesn’t keep units off it; if the route over it is still under more fire, units won’t take it, and the Sabotage step warns you. From 25 coins, at most 1 a wave.

Knock-out. Goes on a tower you’ve seen; tap it for its card, its price and the knock-out cap. Its sapper stops it shooting for the rest of the wave the moment it walks into the tower’s range; units keep their route (only a bridge landing changes it). It hits the spot: a tower moved away is safe. It costs 15 plus 20% of the tower’s slider price (its tuning, not its build fee), and the towers you target in one wave can add up to 50% of the slider prices of all the towers you saw; one tower always fits.

Each bridge costs 25% of its base more for every bridge you sent in earlier waves, and each knock-out 25% more for every earlier knock-out, landed or not. Targets in the same wave don’t raise each other’s price.

No sabotage in the first or the final wave. With Sabotage off on the start screen there is none at all: the attacker has no Sabotage step, and units get more health for the same price. There’s no move fee either: the defender moves towers free all match.
